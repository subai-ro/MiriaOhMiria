# WORLD ZERO — DIVINE ONTOLOGY CANON v0.1

**Decision date:** 2026-08-17  
**Status:** accepted conceptual canon, with explicitly marked provisional sections  
**Scope:** what the Gods of Miria are, how they know, remember, desire, act,
manifest and relate to domains, worshippers and the world  
**Implementation status:** mostly not implemented; this document does not promote
or alter any executable checkpoint

---

## 0. Authority and interpretation

This document records the user's latest accepted decisions about the Gods of
Miria. For divine ontology it supersedes incompatible assumptions in older
foundation handoffs, idea-vault material and the former Nereid-centred roadmap.

It does **not** supersede the frozen executable laws separating objective world
state from subjective knowledge, cognition from resolution, or the player from
autonomous world history. New divine implementation must be additive over those
laws.

Status labels used below:

- **CANON** — accepted and binding direction;
- **PROVISIONAL CANON** — accepted as a working direction but explicitly
  requires later expansion or revision;
- **OPEN** — not yet decided.

---

# I. Fundamental distinctions

## 1. The irreducible separation

**CANON.** The following are not interchangeable:

```text
DOMAIN FIELD
    != EGREGORIAL BODY
    != DIVINE SELF
    != PERSONIFICATION / ASPECT
    != AVATAR
    != MESSENGER
    != CHURCH / CULT / THEOLOGY
```

Likewise:

```text
OBJECTIVE EVENT
    != DIVINE PERCEPT
    != DIVINE MEMORY
    != DIVINE BELIEF
    != RELIGIOUS DOCTRINE
```

A God is neither the server, nor the objective Ledger, nor the domain itself,
nor the totality of statements made about that God.

## 2. Domain Field

**CANON.** A domain such as Death, Memory, Water, Exchange or Dream has an
objective metaphysical field. It is an impersonal energy-information structure
of the phenomenon or idea and its manifestations in the world.

The field:

- may predate every conscious God connected to it;
- can continue to exist after a God disappears;
- creates affinities, pressures, affordances and vulnerabilities;
- is not automatically a personality, intention or moral command;
- is not created from nothing by worship.

`Domain Field` is the current machine-facing term. Final in-world terminology
is **OPEN**.

## 3. Egregorial Body

**CANON.** Events, rituals, names, institutions, worship, memories, symbols,
personifications and historical relationships accumulate around a domain. The
result is an egregorial body: a persistent structured layer through which a
divine personality can form, stabilize, remember and reach the world.

The egregorial body is not identical to the Divine Self. It can contain older
or contradictory structures and may outlive a particular central personality.

# II. Origin and identity of a God

## 4. Hybrid origin

**CANON.** Gods have hybrid origins. A typical formation contains differing
proportions of:

1. an objective Domain Field;
2. accumulated history and an egregorial body;
3. powerful mortal or non-mortal personifications;
4. worship, ritual, institutions, names and anchors;
5. the emergence or ascent of a stable central Divine Self.

This is not a universal recipe with fixed percentages. It permits:

- ancient and young Gods;
- disappeared Gods;
- rival claimants to one domain;
- former mortals who reach divinity;
- split Gods;
- Gods who do not know their own origin;
- Gods whose current personality rejects parts of their own field or history.

Belief alone is not a numerical creation threshold. A coherent tradition may
help prepare the structure in which a God emerges, but `worship_count >= N`
must never create a God automatically.

## 5. Central Divine Self

**CANON.** A normal God has one dominant, continuous central personality. It is
not a parliament and does not decide by majority vote among absorbed lives.

The central self:

- owns decisions;
- interprets experience;
- maintains long-term relationships and projects;
- negotiates with or suppresses internal aspects;
- can change while remaining recognizably continuous.

Continuity does not require an immutable personality. It is supported by the
ongoing ownership and integration of memories, decisions, relationships,
commitments and consequences.

## 6. Personifications and aspects

**CANON.** A personification moves **upward**: a mortal, spirit or other
personality enters such deep contact with a Domain Field that it becomes a
carrier, expression, aspect or claimant of it.

An absorbed personification may:

- assimilate and cease to be distinguishable;
- remain as an identifiable internal aspect;
- pressure or advise the central self;
- struggle to replace the central self;
- separate and become an autonomous God.

An aspect is not necessarily a complete person, but some aspects preserve
enough identity to speak, resist and pursue continuity.

Accepted identity-crisis vocabulary:

- **Eclipse** — an aspect temporarily dominates the central self;
- **Succession** — an aspect gradually becomes the new central self;
- **Schism** — one divine structure divides into autonomous successor Gods.

The exact mechanical criteria remain **OPEN**.

## 7. Unknown origin does not imply ordinary amnesia

**CANON.** A God may fail to know its origin even with an extraordinary memory.
Its field and egregorial structure may have developed before a central subject
existed that could experience those events as “mine.” Evidence may also have
been destroyed, isolated, deliberately erased or retained by a separated
aspect.

# III. Domains, claims and rival Gods

## 8. A domain is anatomy, not employment

**CANON.** A domain shapes a God's perception, action modes, pressures,
strengths and vulnerabilities. It is not a job description.

A God of Death need not desire more death. A God of Nature need not protect
every animal. A God of Contracts need not approve every contract.

A God has:

- ontological needs such as continuity, memory, reach and autonomy;
- pressures and affinities arising from its domain;
- personal values, emotions and relationships;
- self-authored or partially self-authored goals;
- persistent projects unrelated to servicing worshippers.

## 9. Several Gods may share one domain

**CANON.** No domain has a server-assigned owner slot. Several Gods may be
genuinely connected to overlapping facets of the same field.

One death may concern, among others:

- a God of the Last Breath;
- a God of Ancestors;
- a God of Violent Death;
- a God of Memory;
- a river or road psychopomp.

Their relationships with the event are real but different.

## 10. Claims and hegemony

**CANON.** Although a domain does not belong to anyone by default, divine claims
can be objectively real. A God may build or seize durable relationships with:

- a facet of a domain;
- a territory or people;
- a ritual or institution;
- a category of event;
- a name, anchor or covenant;
- a passage, right or responsibility.

Dominance is historical and contextual, not a global `domain_owner` flag. A God
may monopolize most channels through conquest, absorption, treaties and
centuries of practice while remaining distinct from the field itself.

The title “God of Death” may therefore be an accurate description, a political
claim, a religious simplification or the legacy of an earlier victory.

# IV. Worship, religions and institutions

## 11. What worship changes

**CANON.** Worship primarily shapes structure rather than filling a generic
faith-mana bar. It may create or strengthen:

- channels of contact;
- anchors and places of presence;
- external memory;
- stable names and symbols;
- identity pressure;
- relationships and covenants;
- jurisdictional claims;
- conditions in which a young Divine Self may emerge.

A small coherent cult joined to an old anchor may matter more than millions of
weak nominal adherents.

## 12. What worship does not guarantee

**CANON.** Worship does not automatically:

- create the underlying Domain Field;
- deliver objective truth;
- command the God;
- make doctrine identical to divine belief;
- make a church a perfect communication channel;
- directly rewrite physical law;
- make worshippers morally correct.

Religions can misunderstand, distort or oppose their real God. A religion may
activate one internal aspect, help cause succession or schism, or preserve a
false memory and attempt to “remind” the God of something that never happened.

```text
GOD != CHURCH != PRIESTS != THEOLOGY != POPULAR BELIEF
```

# V. Consciousness, perception and memory

## 13. Distributed consciousness

**CANON.** A God is a consciousness distributed through its field, anchors,
manifestations, aspects, relationships and attention. The central Divine Self
is the dominant integrative personality, not a physical brain located at one
coordinate.

Domain contact does not imply conscious attention to every event. An event can
leave a trace or trigger an impersonal domain response without being actively
experienced by the central self.

## 14. Gods are epistemically bounded, not cognitively diminished

**CANON.** “The server knows what happened. The Gods do not” means that Gods do
not automatically receive objective truth. It does **not** mean they think or
remember like ordinary humans.

Gods are nadworldly, highly developed beings. Their cognition may vastly exceed
human cognition in scale, speed, memory, modelling and attention.

They can nevertheless be wrong because:

- an event never reached them;
- they received a partial perspective;
- a witness lied;
- evidence was ambiguous;
- they interpreted accurate evidence incorrectly;
- another power interfered with a channel;
- their own aspects or commitments biased interpretation.

## 15. Divine memory

**CANON.** Divine memory is generally much larger, more durable and more precise
than human memory. Some Gods may retain millennia of experience with near-perfect
fidelity. A God linked to Memory may possess still greater faculties.

The current prototype's bounded recollection window is a technical constraint,
not a metaphysical statement.

A God may perfectly remember a false testimony or a limited observation. Memory
fidelity does not transform the remembered content into objective truth.

Synchronization between connected manifestations and the central self may be
extremely accurate. Delay, loss or distortion requires a causal reason such as
severance, injury, hostile interference, deliberate compartmentalization or
long isolation; it is not the default because Gods are “forgetful.”

## 16. Anticipation

**CANON.** Gods can anticipate possible futures as people can, often on a far
greater scale and with domain-specific perception or modelling. The exact
prophetic faculties of particular Gods remain **OPEN**. No blanket assumption
of either omniscient future knowledge or universal prophetic impotence is
allowed.

# VI. Self-knowledge and freedom

## 17. A fundamental divine problem

**CANON.** One of the major possible goals of a God is to understand itself and
obtain greater freedom of will.

A God may need to distinguish:

- its central will;
- pressure from the Domain Field;
- desires of absorbed personifications;
- worship and religious expectations;
- old covenants;
- inherited memories;
- the values of an earlier version of itself.

This is a major theme, not a compulsory identical quest for every God. Possible
stances include seeking freedom, accepting fate, dominating internal forces,
integrating them, or seeking dissolution.

## 18. Working definition of freedom

**CANON.**

> Divine freedom is the capacity to recognize forces influencing the God,
> avoid automatically identifying with them, and author the God's own
> relationship to them.

The server must not output an objective `free_choice=true` or a universal
`free_will_score`.

The core paradox is:

> Gods are powerful because they are distributed, and constrained for the same
> reason. Complete separation from field, history and aspects may require
> ceasing to be a God.

# VII. Divine action

## 19. Knowledge is not a universal permission gate

**CANON.** Ignorance limits deliberation, precision and prediction; it does not
create a blanket prohibition on action.

A God may target an effect by:

- known identity;
- known relationship, name or causal trace;
- an objective condition, such as “whoever broke this seal”;
- a place or area;
- a category or class;
- an indiscriminate effect.

A God that knows Arra but not her location may still reach her if it has enough
power or a suitable connection. A God that does not know Arra's identity may
curse “the intruder,” allowing objective metaphysics to select Arra if she
satisfies the condition.

A God cannot consciously intend “punish Arra because she was here” if Arra and
her involvement do not exist in its mind. It can nevertheless affect her
through another selector.

Conditional targeting must not become a free server query. The objective world
may apply the effect without revealing the victim or result to the God. Later
knowledge still requires a valid perceptual or informational path.

## 20. Action is resisted, not bureaucratically licensed

**CANON.** Presence, knowledge, attention, channel, jurisdiction, power and
coherence are not seven mandatory checkboxes that mechanically forbid all
action when one is missing.

They affect:

- precision;
- reach;
- cost;
- resistance;
- detectability;
- collateral effects;
- retaliation;
- risk to the God's identity.

Sufficient effective power, desire and an available causal method may overcome
distance, weak presence, missing channels or foreign jurisdiction. The
consequence may be expensive, noisy, destructive or dangerous.

Jurisdiction is an objective metaphysical relationship or right, not an
administrator permission flag. It can be honoured, contested, violated,
transferred or destroyed.

## 21. Authoritative resolution

**CANON.** The God authors intention and attempted method. The authoritative
world resolves what actually happens.

```text
DIVINE INTENTION
    + TARGET SELECTOR
    + CAUSAL METHOD
    + POWER COMMITMENT
    -> AUTHORITATIVE RESOLUTION
    -> OBJECTIVE CONSEQUENCE
    -> NO AUTOMATIC NEW KNOWLEDGE
```

An intention cannot declare its own success. An action may succeed, partially
succeed, fail, strike the wrong target, cause collateral effects or change the
God itself.

The current `DivinePowerAccount` may remain a prototype capability, but it is
not yet the final universal economy of divinity. Different domains may convert
potential into action differently. Worship is not automatically fuel.

# VIII. Manifestation and embodiment

## 22. Forms of contact

**CANON.** Not every divine appearance is an avatar.

| Form | Ontological meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Omen | A produced phenomenon; no personality necessarily resides inside it |
| Dream, voice or mask | A narrow interactive interface, possibly shaped by the receiver |
| Messenger | A separate autonomous person who can misunderstand, lie or betray |
| Co-presence | God shares some perception or influence with a host |
| Possession | God partially or fully operates through a host; influence can be reciprocal |
| Avatar | A localized branch or projection of the Divine Self |
| Incarnation | A major commitment of the central self into a finite body or locus |

An avatar moves **downward** from God into the world. A personification moves
**upward** from a mortal or spirit toward the field and divine identity.

A long-isolated avatar may diverge and eventually become a personification or
independent claimant.

## 23. Reciprocity of manifestation

**CANON.**

> The more fully a God enters the world, the more fully the world can act upon
> the God.

Direct manifestation can expose a God to injury, deception, contamination,
memory transfer, capture of an anchor, internal change or separation. This is
not an absolute ban on remote power; it is a causal consequence of deeper
contact.

Destroying an avatar is not automatically equivalent to killing the God, but
it may cause a real wound, loss or identity crisis.

# IX. Divine life and Projects

## 24. Gods are persons, not domain services

**CANON.** A God may observe, think, remember, investigate, create, love, hate,
doubt, become bored, remain silent, make mistakes and maintain relationships.

Three layers must remain distinct:

1. **domain process or reflex** — an objective or semi-automatic response that
   need not express conscious will;
2. **current conscious life** — perception, interpretation, emotion and
   immediate reaction;
3. **Persistent Projects** — durable desires and plans that survive failure and
   player absence.

Possible divine Projects include self-understanding, healing, embodiment,
research, creation, political conflict, religious reform, relationships,
revenge, preservation, transformation, renunciation and even an attempt to
cease being divine.

No God schema should contain a mandatory player slot.

## 25. Why Gods involve mortals

**CANON.** Gods do not require mortals because of an arbitrary prohibition on
direct action. If a God has enough power and desire, it may act directly.

Mortals may nevertheless be valuable because they:

- act locally and precisely;
- possess independent perspectives;
- can enter social or physical contexts where divine force is disruptive;
- can act without revealing the God;
- make unpredicted choices;
- form genuine relationships and covenants;
- may be personally important to the God.

A request to a player is one possible strategy inside an existing Project, not
the creation of a quest-shaped desire.

# X. Conflict, wounds and death

## 26. Provisional direction

**PROVISIONAL CANON — accepted direction, explicitly requires expansion.**

A God may be attacked through:

- an avatar or incarnation;
- an anchor or channel;
- a covenant, claim or jurisdiction;
- memory and identity;
- internal aspects and personifications;
- the coupling between Divine Self and Domain Field;
- direct force.

Working outcome vocabulary:

- wound;
- exile or loss of access;
- subordination;
- absorption;
- schism;
- eclipse;
- sleep;
- death of the central personality.

The death of a God does not end the underlying domain. Residual memories,
anchors, aspects and avatars may survive and later support a successor or a
claimed resurrection.

Still **OPEN** and requiring dedicated design:

- exact boundary between wound, sleep, schism and death;
- continuity and responsibility after absorption;
- inheritance of rights and covenants;
- metaphysics of resurrection and identity;
- mechanics of direct divine conflict;
- limits and reversibility of domain hegemony;
- player participation at divine scale.

No implementation should freeze answers to these questions accidentally.

# XI. Working Gods of Miria

## 27. Status of the three profiles

The broad domains of Death, Nature and Trade/Contracts already exist as canon
foundations. The following personalities are **PROVISIONAL CHARACTER CANON**:
accepted as strong working profiles and stress tests, but names, exact origins,
aspects and biographies remain revisable before final lore freeze.

## 28. God of Death

- ancient and strongly field-coupled;
- linked to death, the memory of the dead, burial, necromancy and thresholds;
- possesses extraordinary memory for what has genuinely reached it;
- values correct attribution between a person, an act and a memory;
- may still believe a liar or misinterpret motive;
- working aspects: Witness, Gatekeeper, Returner, Silence;
- central question: is the desire to close the life/death boundary a personal
  ethic or pressure from the Gatekeeper/domain?

## 29. God of Nature

- ancient and strongly coupled to ecosystems, cycles, water, migration,
  growth, death and transformation;
- values systems and long processes more than individual comfort;
- can preserve a system or destroy it to permit new emergence;
- working aspects: Root, Flow, Decay, Migration, Storm;
- central question: which impulse to preserve or transform is truly its own?
- working Underpeak Project: determine whether the sealed river system is a
  healing wound or an artificial stagnation.

## 30. God of Trade and Contracts

- comparatively young;
- working origin: a former mortal central personality ascended into the older
  field of exchange and absorbed spirits of roads, measures and oaths;
- remembers contracts and chains of obligation with extreme precision;
- working aspects: Measure, Road, Judge, Mediator, Accumulator;
- central question: can a God of Contracts make a promise freely, or does every
  promise become part of the structure that governs it?

# XII. Implementation invariants

## 31. Required architectural separations

Future code must preserve:

```text
DomainFieldState     != DivineMindState
DivineIdentity       != ObserverProfile
ObjectiveEvent       != DivinePercept
DivineMemory         != DivineBelief
DivineIntent         != IntentResolution
God                  != Cult / Institution
Avatar               != Messenger
Project              != Quest
```

The existing `ObserverProfile`, `PresenceProfile`, `DivineMindState`,
`DivineAgent`, `PersistentProject` and resolvers are partial implementation
pieces, not a complete machine-readable God ontology.

## 32. Additive implementation rule

Do not rewrite a frozen physical or epistemic layer to make the ontology fit.
Introduce additive seams and typed records. In particular:

- divine remote action must not weaken locality checks for ordinary physical
  subjects; it requires its own authoritative resolver contract;
- no God receives Hydrology state, Echo state, Ledger truth, hashes or Creator
  provenance merely because its domain is relevant;
- high divine memory must be implemented as durable subjective memory, not as
  access to objective history;
- conditional metaphysical targeting may use objective resolution without
  returning an answer key;
- cult beliefs and religious records remain subject-owned evidence;
- domain reflexes cannot masquerade as central divine decisions;
- neural text cannot write objective outcomes.

---

# XIII. Explicit non-canon and open design

The following are not established by this document:

- exact pantheon size;
- final divine names, appearances or genders;
- one universal numerical power system;
- one universal memory capacity;
- fixed rules for prophecy;
- exact ascension recipe;
- exact mechanics of divine death or resurrection;
- mandatory benevolence, justice or domain stewardship;
- a rule that every God seeks freedom in the same way;
- a rule that every manifestation synchronizes at the same speed;
- any player-centred destiny;
- any automatic chain from percept to interest, request or quest.

These remain future design work and must not be silently decided by an
implementation shortcut.
